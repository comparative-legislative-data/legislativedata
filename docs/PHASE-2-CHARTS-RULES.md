# The charts' rules, after the step back

For the owner, 17 September 2026. The downloads stay plain, and the charts and
tables may bring the data alive (`DECISIONS.md`, 17 September). Three rules
were applied to the charts as if they were downloads. Each is below: what it
says now, and a proposal for what replaces it.

**All three settled by the owner, 17 September, as proposed**, with choices made
through filters and dropdowns. Recorded in `DECISIONS.md`.

---

## 1. Colour

**Now** (15 September). An outcome is a word, never a colour. The accent,
which means "you can act here", never touches data.

**Proposed.**
- **On a chart, colour may tell things apart**: the outcomes in a stacked bar,
  or the sessions as lines. A chart palette of its own, readable by someone
  with colour-blindness, and in both dark and light.
- **Colour never stands alone.** Every colour has its word beside it, in a
  label or a legend, and its figure. A reader who cannot tell two colours apart
  loses nothing.
- **Colour does not grade.** No green for passed and red for fell. A Member's
  Bill falling is an ordinary outcome, and the chart should not say otherwise.
  This is the one part of the old rule proposed to stay, and it is your call.
- **Unchanged:** the accent never touches data, so nothing on a chart looks
  like a button. In the downloads, and in the table of every bill, an outcome
  is a word.

## 2. What a reader can choose

**Now** (17 September). Two switches: leaving out bills of a given kind, and
whether an average takes every bill that reached a stage or only those that
passed. No other.

**Proposed.**
- **Each chart gives the choices its write-up justifies.** From your thoughts:
  a type of bill, or all; a session; the stretch of a bill's passage measured.
- **The choices are a fixed list we set, not a chart builder.** That keeps to
  `PLAN.md`, which leaves tools for readers to build their own charts to the
  playground.
- **The two existing switches stay available** to any chart that needs them.

## 3. How a chart is drawn

**Now** (17 September). Drawn on the machine when the copy is taken, as a
finished image, with its numbers in a table beneath. Nothing is drawn in the
reader's browser.

**Proposed.**
- **Drawn in the reader's browser**, so a reader can hover over a bar for its
  figure and a choice redraws the chart at once.
- **The numbers are still worked out in the database**, for every choice, when
  the copy is taken. The page only draws. So what a reader sees and what they
  download cannot drift apart.
- **This needs a charting library**, which is an addition. It is named and put
  to you before it is used, not now.
- **Two settled points are restated, not dropped:**
  - **The date.** It was to sit inside the saved image. Now it sits inside the
    chart's own frame, so a screenshot still carries it.
  - **A reader who cannot see the chart.** The table beneath still holds every
    number. The one-sentence description moves from the image onto the chart
    itself.

---

## What stays exactly as it is

- **Every download**, and everything decided about it.
- **Every number is worked out in the database**, and is the number a reader
  downloads.
- **From a figure to its bills, and from a bill to its provenance.**
- **Every chart names the methodology notes it rests on, its sources and its
  date**, and has its numbers beneath it as a table and a CSV.
- **The page test**: could a reader see where this came from, when, and what we
  do not know; and is anything here to impress rather than to inform.
