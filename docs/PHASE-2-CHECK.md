# Phase 2's plan, checked

Checked on 16 September by a session that wrote none of `docs/PHASE-2.md`.
I read it against `PLAN.md`, `STANDING.md`, the decisions it rests on, the
methodology notes and the database.

This is for you. Each finding says what the plan says, what is wrong with it,
and what I propose. Every proposal is written into the plan's second draft,
marked *(check n)*, for you to accept or strike there. This file goes when the
phase is swept.

**What held.** Every item `STANDING.md` leaves for Phase 2 is placed, and placed
somewhere it can be answered. Nothing in the plan builds ahead of the
groundwork. Nothing contradicts what access becomes after beta. Every claim the
plan makes about the data is true:
- no bill lacks an introduction date, no completed stage lacks its date, and no
  Act lacks its Royal Assent date;
- the sessions' dates are right, including Session 5 ending on 4 May 2021;
- the four bills stopped before Royal Assent and the two Robin Rigg bills are as
  the plan describes them;
- five bills of 470 have a recorded procedure.

---

## 1. The order: two questions sit after the things they decide

**What the plan says.** The published copy comes first. Research on downloads
comes second. Whether the site reads files or a database comes last, in the
fourth piece.

**The problem.** Files or a database is the question of what the published copy
*is*. If the answer is files, most of the first piece changes: how it is
refreshed, what the site's way in is, whether there is anything to exclude from
the backup. Deciding it last means deciding the first piece twice.

The 15 September decision also said files or a database waits on what a
reader's tools are, and that is the download research. The plan already lets the
first piece leave its layout question open until that research is read. That
breaks its own rule that each piece is decided before the next opens.

The download research needs nothing from the first piece.

**Proposal.** Run the download research first. Then the published copy, with
files or a database decided inside it. Then the charts and tables. Then how the
working is shown. The order was your decision on 16 September, so this is yours
to change or keep. The reason is above.

## 2. The calculations already built are not mentioned

**What the plan says.** The first piece asks what crosses into the published
copy: bills, stage dates, notes. The third says every chart's calculation is
written out afresh.

**The problem.** The working database already works out the days between each
stage, the time from introduction to final stage, and averages by session and
type. It also counts outcomes by type. Your 13 September ruling is built into
these, so time is counted to every stage the Parliament decided, whichever way
it went. The plan never says whether these cross, or whether a chart's
calculation is built on them or written again. Written again means two versions
of the same arithmetic that can drift apart.

Nor does anything ask whether a download carries the worked-out days or only the
dates. `PLAN.md`'s vision has a researcher downloading "the variables built
from it".

Then there is 15 September: no duration is stored, and every one is worked out
when asked for. The first piece's question 4 asks whether chart figures are
worked out in advance and stored, without mentioning that rule.

**Proposal.** The first piece adds a question: which of the existing
calculations cross, and whether a chart's calculation is built on them. The
download research adds another: whether a download carries worked-out days as
well as dates. Question 4 names the 15 September rule, and asks whether it holds
for the published copy as it does for the working one.

## 3. Your ruling on counting time is misworded

**What the plan says.** One switch is whether an average takes only bills that
passed, "or every bill that completed the stage in question even if it went no
further".

**The problem.** In the database, "completed" means the bill got through the
stage. Your ruling of 13 September, and M2, count every stage the Parliament
decided, whichever way. Taken at its word, the plan's switch leaves out every
bill rejected at Stage 1. Those are all Members' Bills, which is the one-sided
exclusion your ruling removed.

**Proposal.** Use M2's words instead: "every bill that reached that stage, or
only the bills that completed their passage".

## 4. Private Bills are presented as open, and M2 has settled them

**What the plan says.** Whether Private Bills sit in the four intervals, by
stage order or apart, "is a decision".

**The problem.** M2 already says Private Bill stages "are compared with a public
bill's stages by their position in the sequence". The database already lines
them up that way. A write-up proposing anything else would contradict a note
readers will be shown.

**Proposal.** Record comparison by position as settled by M2. What is left is
only whether a chart shows Private Bills with the rest or on their own.

## 5. The test that proves the dataset is not in the plan

**What the plan says.** Nothing.

**The problem.** `STANDING.md`, from 12 September: the dataset is proved at the
end, and the test that counts is whether the charts and tables built from all
seven sessions match the figures you built by hand from the PhD dataset. Phase 2
is when those charts first exist.

**Proposal.** Where a figure of yours exists for a chart, the chart's write-up
names it. Whether the comparison is a condition of closing Phase 2 is yours to
say.

## 6. The financial resolution is due to come back, and the plan doesn't say so

**What the plan says.** Nothing.

**The problem.** Leaving out the financial resolution was parked with a stated
trigger: Stage 1 to Stage 2 figures that cannot be explained without it. The
time-to-pass chart is the first thing that produces those figures.

**Proposal.** That chart's write-up names the trigger, so an odd Stage 1 to
Stage 2 figure gets recognised as the trigger rather than explained away.

## 7. The notes go public, and some are known to be imperfect

**What the plan says.** Each chart names the methodology notes it rests on.

**The problem.** Several of your open items become public the moment a note is
published:
- M5's two drifted sentences;
- whether the other notes should be cut the way M12 was;
- the Robin Rigg Act's incomplete note;
- whether Session 5's four bills that ran out of time get Session 6's note.

**And one I found while checking.** M5 says three bills "were referred under
section 33 and ruled against on 6 October 2021". That date is right for the
UNCRC Bill and the European Charter Bill. For the Legal Continuity Bill it isn't.
The Supreme Court ruled on that reference on 13 December 2018 (*[2018] UKSC 64*,
its own case page, supremecourt.uk/cases/uksc-2018-0080), three years earlier. The bills themselves are recorded
correctly:

| Bill | Passed | Ruling recorded |
|---|---|---|
| European Charter of Local Self-Government (Incorporation) | 23 March 2021 | 6 October 2021 |
| UNCRC (Incorporation) | 16 March 2021 | 6 October 2021 |
| UK Withdrawal from the EU (Legal Continuity) | 21 March 2018 | none: the fact sheet states no date |

So the data is right and the note overreaches. You are the authority here.
Check it against the judgment before anything changes.

**Proposal.** The plan says no note is published with an open question against
it. The items above are settled before the build, each wording change put to you
in full.

## 8. The download research names the formats the drafting session favoured

**What the plan says.** "Where to look" names two particular formats, CSV on the
Web and the Frictionless Data Package, "not as a conclusion".

**The problem.** The drafting session's notes say it favoured exactly those two,
and meant to keep that out of the plan. Naming only those two points the research
at them. The other places it names (the UK Data Service, ICPSR, the comparable
datasets) are fine, because they are places to look rather than answers.

**Proposal.** Drop the two format names and keep the question: formats that
carry a description alongside the table. The research finds the candidates.

## 9. Where a switch ends and the playground begins

**What the plan says.** A reader gets switches: the two you named, and any a
write-up proposes.

**The problem.** `PLAN.md` says Phase 2 must not take on tools for readers to
build their own tables and charts. Your two switches are clearly on the right
side of that. Nothing stops a write-up proposing more until a chart becomes a
chart builder.

**Proposal.** A write-up offers your two switches, plus any a methodology note
requires, such as M9's two ways of measuring the Robin Rigg Act. Anything else
is named in the write-up as belonging to the playground, and not built.

## 10. Three smaller things

- **The style test against a table of every bill.** The plan says it can't be
  done as written, and leaves the alternative open. It should give the briefing
  both limits. Nothing runs on your Mac. And a page with every bill on it, once
  deployed, is data on the site, so either it goes behind something only you can
  see, or it waits for the rules for publishing.
- **Which date a page carries.** Three decisions of 15 September already speak
  of "the date it was taken". The first piece's question 5 should start from
  those words, not from a blank.
- **What the date means, in words a reader sees.** `PLAN.md` wants this for
  charts as well as downloads. The plan only asks it of downloads, in the second
  piece. It should ask it for both.
