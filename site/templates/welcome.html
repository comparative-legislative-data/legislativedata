Scottish Parliament

A research resource for data about the legislative process

Every bill introduced in the Scottish Parliament, what happened to it at each stage, and when — assembled by hand from the Parliament’s own records, with every figure traceable back to what said so.

The Parliament publishes a great deal about its own legislation, and almost none of it is in a form you can count. Dates sit in the prose of a fact sheet; outcomes are described rather than coded; a bill that ran out of time at dissolution is recorded the same way as one that was voted down. Anyone who has tried to answer a straightforward question about how the Parliament legislates has had to rebuild the data first.

This site is that data, built once and published openly. The intention is that a researcher can download the raw records, download the variables built from them, see the whole thing visualised, and build their own tables and charts here without leaving the site.

Nothing is asserted without a route back to its source. Where the record is incomplete, or where a judgement had to be made, the site says so and says who made it. You are free to accept those judgements or to take the data and make your own.
